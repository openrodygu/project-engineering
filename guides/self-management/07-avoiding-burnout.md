# Avoiding burnout

Burnout is not a sign that you need to try harder. It is what happens when demand stays high and recovery stays low for too long.

## Early signs

- Work that used to be easy takes much longer.
- You are tired after a normal day, not just a hard one.
- You feel detached from work you used to care about.
- Small setbacks feel disproportionate.
- You are sick more often, or sleeping badly.
- You keep saying "after this push" and the push never ends.

Notice the pattern early. It is easier to adjust one habit than to recover from months of depletion.

## The usual causes

- **Too much work, too few people.** A planning problem, not a personal failing.
- **No control.** You are responsible for outcomes but cannot influence decisions.
- **Unclear expectations.** You never know when you are done.
- **No recovery.** Evenings and weekends are not protected.
- **Low support.** Problems are yours alone.

Fix the cause where you can. A vacation does not fix an impossible workload.

## Keep a sustainable pace

- Plan for a realistic day, not a perfect one.
- Leave buffer in the week.
- Take breaks before you need them.
- Protect at least one day a week without work.
- Use your time off.

A short, consistent pace delivers more than a series of crashes.

## Have the conversation

If the workload is not sustainable, say so with facts:

> "I have three commitments due next week and room for two. Which one should move?"

Bring options, not just a complaint. If the answer is always "do all of it", that is a decision above your level, and it needs to be named.

## Recovery is not a reward

Rest is part of doing the work, not something you earn after finishing everything. There is always more work. Waiting for a clear desk means never resting.

## When to get help

If exhaustion, anxiety, or low mood persists, talk to a doctor or a professional. That is not a performance problem, and it is not something to manage alone.

## Try it

Write down:

1. One sign of strain you have noticed in yourself.
2. One cause you can influence.
3. One thing you will drop, delegate, or postpone this week.
4. One recovery habit you will protect.

Then have one honest conversation with the person who can change the workload.
