# Boundaries and energy

Focus is easier when other people know when you are available. Boundaries are not walls. They are agreements that protect the time you need to do the work.

## Make availability visible

- Put focus blocks on your calendar and treat them like meetings.
- Tell your team when you will check messages, for example at 11:00 and 15:00.
- Use a status message during deep work.
- Say when you are done for the day, and mean it.

People interrupt less when they know when you will respond. Silence creates anxiety; a stated window creates trust.

## Protect the start and the end

The first hour and the last hour are easy to give away. They are also useful:

- **Start:** plan the day, do one important thing before the inbox.
- **End:** close tasks, write tomorrow's top three, stop.

If you answer email before you plan, someone else has chosen your day.

## Energy is not just time

You can have an hour and no focus. Notice when your energy is best and put the hardest work there.

| Energy | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| High | Deep work, hard decisions, writing |
| Medium | Meetings, review, collaboration |
| Low | Admin, email, routine updates |

A short break is cheaper than an hour of forced focus. Walk, eat, or step away from the screen.

## Say no with a reason and an option

A boundary without an alternative feels like rejection. Try:

> "I cannot take that this week. I can review it on Monday, or Sam can cover it sooner."

That keeps the relationship and the work moving.

## Boundaries with yourself

- Do not start a new task in the last ten minutes of a block.
- Do not check messages during deep work.
- Do not work through lunch every day.
- Do not answer non-urgent messages after hours.

The hardest boundary is usually the one you break for yourself.

## Try it

Write two boundaries for next week:

1. One time boundary, such as "No meetings before 10:00 on Tuesday."
2. One attention boundary, such as "No chat during the 09:00 focus block."

Tell one person about each. A boundary nobody knows about is a preference.
