# Daily and weekly reviews

Reviews are how a task list stays trustworthy. Without them, overdue items pile up and the plan becomes noise.

## The daily close

Five to ten minutes at the end of the day:

1. Open the **Task List**.
2. Close what is done.
3. Reschedule what slipped. Do not leave it overdue.
4. Cancel what is no longer real.
5. Pick tomorrow's top three.
6. Write one sentence about what moved or what got in the way.

The goal is to start tomorrow with a clear list, not to account for every minute.

## The weekly reset

Twenty to thirty minutes once a week:

1. Open the **Task List** and **Calendar**.
2. Clear overdue items: reschedule, do, or cancel.
3. Review `waiting` items and follow up.
4. Look at the week ahead. Is it realistic?
5. Archive completed tasks.
6. Promote anything that has become team work to the team vault.
7. Pick the top three for Monday.

Do it at the same time each week. A review that depends on motivation does not happen.

## Questions worth asking

- What actually got done this week?
- What took longer than expected, and why?
- What am I avoiding?
- What is on the list that no longer matters?
- Where is the next week overcommitted?

The answers are more useful than a perfect checklist.

## Keep the review short

A review that takes two hours will be skipped. A twenty-minute review you do every week beats an elaborate system you abandon.

If the list is huge, clean the top layer only. You do not need to process every old item to start the week well.

## Try it

Run a weekly reset now:

- Reschedule or cancel three overdue tasks.
- Pick three tasks for Monday.
- Write one sentence about what you are avoiding.

Put the next review on your calendar as a recurring task.
