# Motivation and growth

People are not motivated by a single lever. They stay engaged when the work has purpose, they have some control over how they do it, and they can see themselves getting better.

## Three things that matter

- **Autonomy** — room to make decisions inside the work.
- **Mastery** — a chance to get better at something that matters.
- **Purpose** — a clear line between the work and something the person cares about.

Pay and fairness matter too, but they are not a substitute for these. A raise does not fix a job with no autonomy.

## Ask, do not assume

What motivates one person may drain another. Ask:

- What part of your work do you want more of?
- What part would you like to hand off?
- What skill do you want to build this year?
- What kind of recognition matters to you?

Then act on the answer where you can, and be honest where you cannot.

## Growth is mostly assignments

Courses help, but people grow through work that stretches them with support. Look for:

- A task slightly beyond their current level.
- A problem with a clear outcome and room to solve it.
- A chance to present, review, or teach.
- A visible owner role on a real deliverable.

Pair the stretch with a checkpoint and someone to ask. A stretch with no support is just stress.

## Recognize specifically

"Great job" is pleasant but forgettable. Name what the person did and why it mattered:

> "You caught the data loss in the import before release and wrote the test that prevents it. That saved us a customer incident."

Specific recognition tells the person what to repeat and tells the team what good looks like.

## Keep the conversation going

Growth is not a once-a-year plan. Revisit it in one-on-ones. Adjust when the work changes. A plan nobody mentions again is a form, not a plan.

## Try it

Write a one-page growth plan for one person:

- One skill to build.
- One assignment that would build it.
- One checkpoint.
- One thing you will do to support it.

Use it as the growth section of your next one-on-one.
