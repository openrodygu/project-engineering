# Decisions

Teams slow down when it is unclear who decides. Teams also make bad calls when only one person ever decides. The fix is to name the mode before the discussion starts.

## Three modes

| Mode | When to use | Who decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I decide** | Urgent, high-impact, or irreversible | You, after listening |
| **We decide** | Tradeoffs affect several people | The group, with one owner |
| **You decide** | The work belongs to someone else | They do, within agreed bounds |

Say the mode out loud: "I will decide this by Thursday, but I want your input first." That one sentence prevents most decision confusion.

## Reversible and irreversible

- **Reversible** decisions should be made quickly by the person closest to the work. A bad call can be undone.
- **Irreversible** decisions deserve more context, more voices, and a written record.

Most decisions are more reversible than they feel.

## Decide with the right input

Ask:

1. Who has the information?
2. Who has to live with the result?
3. Who has to carry it out?

Consult the people who fit those questions. Do not consult everyone by default. A decision made by twelve people is often a decision made by nobody.

## Write it down

For any decision that would surprise someone later, record:

- The context.
- The options considered.
- The decision.
- The consequences.
- The owner and date.

A short note in the team vault or repository is enough. The point is to preserve the reasoning, not to produce a document.

## Disagree and commit

People will not always agree. Once the decision is made, the team should support it or raise a new fact. Silent non-compliance is worse than an honest objection.

If someone strongly disagrees, hear them out, then decide. If the decision is reversible, offer to revisit it after a short trial.

## Try it

Write a decision log with three recent decisions. For each one, note the mode (I, we, or you decided), the main tradeoff, and whether it was reversible. Then pick one upcoming decision and name the mode before the next discussion.
