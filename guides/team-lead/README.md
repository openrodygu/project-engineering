# Team lead

Help a group of people do good work without taking over their work.

## Do this now

20–30 minutes. You will finish with a one-on-one agenda and a delegation brief.

1. Pick one person you work with.
2. Write a one-on-one agenda with these sections:
   - **Their items** — what they want to discuss first.
   - **Status and blockers** — what is moving and what is stuck.
   - **Feedback** — one thing going well, one thing to adjust.
   - **Growth** — one skill or goal they are working on.
   - **Actions** — who does what before the next meeting.
3. Pick one piece of work you currently own that someone else could do.
4. Write a delegation brief with:
   - The outcome, not just the task.
   - Why it matters.
   - Constraints and deadline.
   - Decisions they can make alone.
   - When you will check in.
5. Use both in your next conversation.

You now have the artifact: an agenda and a brief you can reuse.

## What it is

A team lead creates the conditions for other people to do their best work. That means three jobs:

- **Direction** — everyone knows what matters and why.
- **People** — each person knows what is expected, how they are doing, and where they are going.
- **System** — work moves, blockers surface, and decisions stick.

It does not mean doing the hardest technical work yourself, attending every meeting, or approving every choice.

## Common mistakes

- **Doing instead of leading.** If you own every hard task, the team cannot grow.
- **Delegating tasks without context.** People need the outcome, constraints, and authority.
- **Saving feedback for review time.** Late feedback is a surprise, not coaching.
- **Avoiding conflict.** Small issues become expensive when they are left alone.
- **Canceling one-on-ones when busy.** Those are the weeks they matter most.
