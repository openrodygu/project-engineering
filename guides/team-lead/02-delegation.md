# Delegation

Delegation is not dumping work. It is giving someone an outcome, the context to pursue it, and the authority to make decisions along the way.

## The delegation brief

Use the same structure every time:

```markdown
## Outcome
Ship a vendor comparison the team can use to choose by 2026-11-20.

## Why
We need a decision before the budget review. The comparison is the input.

## Constraints
- Three vendors, same criteria for each.
- Use the template in the project note.
- No vendor contact without approval.

## Your call
- Which sources to use.
- How to score the options.
- When to ask for help.

## Checkpoints
- Draft criteria by Tuesday.
- Full draft by Friday.
- Final review with me on Monday.

## Done means
The comparison is in the team vault, linked from the project, and ready to discuss.
```

The brief answers the questions people are usually afraid to ask.

## Delegate outcomes, not steps

"Make the calls and fill in the form" is a task. "Choose a vendor and explain why" is an outcome. Outcomes give people room to solve the problem their way.

Be explicit about the boundary:

- **They decide** — day-to-day choices inside the outcome.
- **We decide together** — scope changes, tradeoffs that affect other teams.
- **I decide** — budget, policy, or commitments you cannot hand off.

## Match the level of support

| Person | Support |
| --- | --- |
| New to the work | Show one example, check in often |
| Experienced | Agree on checkpoints, stay out of the way |
| Stretched | Clear outcome, available for questions |

Support is not the same as supervision. The more capable the person, the less you should hover.

## Follow through

- Give the deadline and the checkpoints in writing.
- Keep the check-ins short.
- Do not reopen decisions they were allowed to make.
- Give credit in public.
- If it goes wrong, review the brief before blaming the person.

## Try it

Write a delegation brief for one item from your "someone else could own this" list. Include the outcome, two constraints, one decision that is theirs, and one checkpoint. Send it or use it in your next conversation.
