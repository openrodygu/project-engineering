# Communication

Decide who needs what, how often, and through which channel before the silence becomes a problem.

## Start with a plan

A communication plan is a small table. It answers four questions for each audience:

| Audience | What they need | Channel | Cadence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sponsor | Decisions, cost, date changes | Short email or 15-minute call | Weekly |
| Team | Priorities, blockers, next work | Standup and task board | Daily |
| Affected teams | Upcoming changes, downtime | Group message | At milestones |
| Stakeholders | Outcome and progress | Written status | Monthly |

Not everyone needs everything. Sending more updates does not fix a missing decision.

## Status updates

A status update is not a diary. It reports change and asks for what is needed.

```markdown
**Status:** On track / At risk / Off track
**Done this week:** ...
**Next week:** ...
**Blockers or decisions needed:** ...
**Date confidence:** High / Medium / Low
```

Rules that work:

- Lead with the state, then the detail.
- Say "at risk" early. Late honesty is not honesty, it is a surprise.
- Name the person who can unblock you.
- Keep it short enough that people read it.

In dotpm, the project board and weekly review are the source. The update summarizes them; it does not replace them.

## Meetings

A meeting is expensive because it costs every attendee at once. Use one when a decision or a discussion needs live exchange.

Before the meeting, write:

1. The purpose.
2. The decision or output you need.
3. The people who must be there.
4. The pre-read.

After the meeting, write the decision and the actions. If there is no decision and no action, the meeting did not need to happen.

## Decisions and escalation

Record decisions where the work lives, not only in chat. A short decision log entry is enough: what was decided, why, who accepted it, and when it takes effect.

Escalate when:

- A decision is outside your authority.
- A blocker has no owner or no response.
- A date or scope change affects a commitment.

Escalate with the trade-off, not just the problem. "We can keep the date or keep the integration, not both" is a useful message. "We are blocked" is not.

## Async by default

Write things down so people can read them on their own schedule. Use live meetings for negotiation, conflict, and shared thinking. Use written updates for status, decisions, and reference.

## Try it

Write a one-page communication plan for your project.

1. List three audiences and what each needs.
2. Pick a channel and cadence for each.
3. Write one status update using the template.
4. Record one decision with its owner and date.

Put the plan and the update in `local-project/pm-vault/`.
