# Project management

Plan work in small, visible pieces so the team knows what done means and what happens next.

## Do this now

Take 15–30 minutes and produce three artifacts. Start with the browser or any notes app. The Obsidian version is optional. If you only have five minutes, do step 1 and stop.

### Example

**Project:** Get the stockroom relabeled before the holiday rush.

- **Goal:** Every shelf has the new label before the holiday sale.
- **Definition of done:** All shelves labeled and spot-checked by the store manager.
- **In scope:** Back room and sales floor shelves.
- **Out of scope:** Warehouse inventory counts.
- **Stakeholders:** Store manager (decision maker), shift leads (contributors), staff (affected).

| Task | Owner | Status | Due |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Print first label batch | Jamie | in progress | Nov 10 |
| Label back room shelves | Shift leads | to do | Nov 12 |
| Spot-check and fix gaps | Store manager | to do | Nov 13 |

**Next action:** Print the first batch of shelf labels tomorrow at 9am.

### Browser or notes-app version (no setup)

1. **Write a one-page project brief.** Create a note or document. Google Docs, Sheets, Asana, or paper all work. Fill in:

   ```markdown
   **Goal:** What changes if this succeeds?

   **Definition of done:**
   - ...
   - ...

   **In scope:** ...
   **Out of scope:** ...
   **Stakeholders:** decision maker, contributors, affected people.
   ```

   Keep it to one screen. A brief you cannot read in a minute is not a brief. Use headings and bullets in your notes app; you do not need to type the asterisks.

2. **Make a small task list.** Add three tasks with an owner, a status, and a due date. A simple table is enough:

   | Task | Owner | Status | Due |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Draft project brief | you | in progress | ... |
   | Review brief with stakeholder | ... | to do | ... |
   | Publish the plan | ... | to do | ... |

   Set a priority only where the consequence is real. A simple list works too; you do not need to type the pipes.

3. **Pick one personal next action.** Write the next physical action you will take and when you will do it.

You now have a team-facing plan and one personal action. That small loop is the whole skill in miniature.

Need a fuller brief later? Use the [project brief template](../../resources/templates/project-brief.md). Planning a kickoff? Use the [project kickoff checklist](../../resources/checklists/project-kickoff.md).

### Obsidian version (optional)

If you use the workspace templates, do the same three steps in Obsidian:

1. Open `local-project/pm-vault/` and create a project in the **Project manager** pane (dotpm). Put the brief in the project’s **Description** field.
2. Add the three tasks to that project. Give each one an assignee, a status, and a due date.
3. Open `local-project/personal-project-notes/` and add one TaskNotes task: the next physical action you will take. Schedule it for today.

The team vault is the shared view. The personal vault is yours. See [Obsidian](../obsidian/README.md) if you are setting up the vaults for the first time.

## What it is

Project management is a loop, not a document:

1. Define the outcome and what done means.
2. Break the outcome into small pieces.
3. Prioritize the pieces.
4. Estimate and sequence them.
5. Execute and track reality.
6. Review, adjust, and close.

The loop repeats. A plan that never changes is usually a plan nobody is using.

The brief is the anchor. Tasks are the work. Status is the truth. Review is what keeps the three connected.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing a brief nobody can finish.** If the definition of done is vague, every task is arguable.
- **Creating tasks with no owner or date.** That is a wish list, not a plan.
- **Treating the tool as the process.** dotpm stores the plan. It does not decide what matters.
- **Keeping two copies of one commitment.** The team vault is authoritative for team work. The personal vault is for your execution.
- **Never reviewing.** A stale board is worse than no board because people trust it.
