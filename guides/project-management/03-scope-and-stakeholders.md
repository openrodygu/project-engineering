# Scope and stakeholders

Write down what is in, what is out, and who gets a say before the work starts.

## Scope

Scope is the boundary of the project. A scope statement has three parts:

- **In scope** — the work you will do.
- **Out of scope** — the work you will not do, stated plainly.
- **Assumptions** — what you are treating as true.

The out-of-scope list is the most useful part. It prevents the slow drift where small requests pile up and the team is blamed for missing a date nobody changed.

```markdown
**In scope:**
- Compare three vendors
- Run one pilot
- Sign a contract

**Out of scope:**
- Building a custom integration
- Migrating existing contracts
- Training every office
```

## Change control

A change is anything that alters scope, date, or quality after the plan is accepted. You do not need a committee. You need a visible decision.

For each change, record:

1. What is changing.
2. Why it is needed now.
3. What it costs in time, scope, or quality.
4. Who accepted the trade-off.

A short note in the project description is enough. The point is that the trade-off is written down, not absorbed silently.

## Stakeholders

A stakeholder is anyone who decides, contributes, or is affected. Not everyone needs the same attention.

A simple map:

| Name | Role | Interest | Influence | What they need |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ana | Sponsor | High | High | Decisions and costs |
| Ben | Contributor | High | Medium | Clear tasks |
| Cara | Affected team | Medium | Low | Advance notice |

Use the map to choose communication, not to label people. High influence and high interest need close involvement. Low influence and low interest need a summary, not a meeting.

## One decision maker

Projects stall when nobody can say yes. Name one decision maker for the outcome. Other people can advise, contribute, and review. One person accepts the result.

If two people share the final say, say so explicitly and define how disagreements are resolved. Ambiguity here becomes a delay later.

## Try it

Write a scope statement and a stakeholder map for your current project.

1. List three items in scope and three out of scope.
2. Name the single decision maker.
3. Map three stakeholders with their interest, influence, and what they need.
4. Add one sentence on how a scope change gets accepted.

Put this in the project description in `local-project/pm-vault/`.
