# Health and metrics

Metrics are useful when they help a team make a decision. They are harmful when they become a scoreboard.

## Four simple areas

### Flow

- **Work in progress** — how many tasks are active at once?
- **Cycle time** — how long does a task take from start to done?
- **Lead time** — how long from request to done?
- **Throughput** — how many tasks finish per week?

### Predictability

- Are due dates met, missed, or repeatedly moved?
- Are estimates close, or consistently wrong in one direction?
- How often does scope change mid-cycle?

### Quality

- How often does work come back after “done”?
- How many blockers are discovered late?
- Are reviews happening before release?

### Team health

- Is work concentrated in one person?
- Are people working outside their hours?
- Are disagreements resolved or avoided?
- Do people know what is expected of them?

## A minimal dashboard

Track only these for one month:

1. Tasks in progress.
2. Tasks done this week.
3. Overdue tasks.
4. Blocked tasks.
5. One quality signal.

That is enough to spot a trend.

## Avoid vanity metrics

- Lines of code.
- Number of commits.
- Hours worked.
- Story points compared across teams.
- Utilization as a target.

These are easy to measure and easy to game. They rarely improve the system.

## Metrics are for learning

Ask:

- What decision will this metric change?
- What would we do differently if it moved?
- Who could be harmed by this measurement?

If there is no answer, do not track it.
